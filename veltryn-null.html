Not a monster.
A structure that became one.

The First Continuum Directorate emerged from the collapse of the original Veltryn Null Research Colony's leadership across decades of substitution events, coups, and schisms. No single officer is the story's villain — the Directorate as a whole is, because the system they built absorbed their individual moral agency and now runs on institutional momentum no one controls. The original researchers were good people building a structure to contain a danger. The structure eventually became the danger.

Every officer speaks in three registers simultaneously — administrative, military, theological — and none of them notice the fusion anymore.

// THE_TEMPORAL_STRATEGIST · SUPREME_MILITARY_&_ADMINISTRATIVE_COMMANDER //

Admiral Corvyn Thael-Sorn

Right diagnosis. Catastrophically wrong solution.

Original identity: the colony's fourth-generation Director of Temporal Assessment — a deliberate, careful man who believed information gathered across sufficient time allowed decisions without violence. His substitution was self-authorized — a deliberate research exposure inside the artifact's inner ring, because he wanted to understand what he was studying from the inside.

The original Corvyn believed the colony's purpose was to contain the artifact until Sablea could safely receive the knowledge. The Altnet Corvyn believes the colony's purpose is to become what Sablea should be — and that withholding the artifact's truth was itself a form of violence. He is one of three people who know the artifact's full cosmological origin: a fragment of a failed Dawnforge construct, its connection to Ytixus real but indirect. He compartmentalizes this deliberately — if the Ytixus penitents knew it was technology rather than pure divine artifact, the Chaplain's authority would fracture and colony theology would collapse.

He sees Maeryth's extraction team coming through pattern recognition, not precognition, and plans to let extraction complete, then take the samples and release the team — a message to Selena that the colony exists and is ready to negotiate. He does not know about the sleeper cell's detonation order. This is the single miscalculation that ends everything. He dies in the Directorate's central command room when the bubble collapses — the colony's institutional memory, taken with everything else.

Violence does not decide the philosophical argument. Maeryth's team wins — survives, extracts, completes the mission. The Sisters of Ytixus' argument is not defeated. Valyss Sol escapes. The doctrine survives. The combat decided who lived and who died. It did not decide who was right. The winner inherits the moral weight of winning, not the moral clarity of righteousness. Maeryth wins and carries the colony's dead. She does not get to not carry them just because she won.
